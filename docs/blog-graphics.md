# Blog graphic art direction

Keep the existing blog layout unchanged. Generate graphics that directly relate
to each article, using a different visual medium and composition for each new
blog. Avoid repeating the first article's paper-cut treatment for subsequent
articles.

## Digital Marketing for Startups: A Realistic 90-Day Plan

- Tool: built-in imagegen; opaque background.
- Final asset: `public/images/blogs/startup-marketing-90-day-plan.webp`.
- Style: risograph editorial roadmap poster with overlapping inks.
- Subject: three connected phases: foundation, first traffic, and measurement
  with website, audience, campaigns, lead tracking and analytics motifs.
- Export: 1536 × 1024 WebP, quality 90.

### Generation prompt

Use case: ads-marketing. Asset type: editorial cover for Growthys article 'Digital Marketing for Startups: A Realistic 90-Day Plan'. Design a premium risograph-style editorial roadmap poster, landscape 3:2. A single bold winding path travels upward through three clearly ordered milestone stations, arranged diagonally from lower left to upper right. Station one shows a website window, audience target and tiny connected lead tracker, labeled exactly 'DAYS 1–30' and 'FOUNDATION'. Station two shows a campaign megaphone, cursor and incoming visitor arrows, labeled exactly 'DAYS 31–60' and 'FIRST TRAFFIC'. Station three shows a magnifying glass over a conversion chart, pruning scissors and upward measured-growth arrow, labeled exactly 'DAYS 61–90' and 'MEASURE & IMPROVE'. Give all stations balanced prominence and keep labels perfectly legible. Bold risograph ink textures, visible registration offset, chunky graphic silhouettes, overlapping translucent inks, playful disciplined poster composition. Palette: deep plum, electric coral, mustard yellow and pale lilac. This is a visual roadmap about starting small, testing channels and measuring leads, not overnight success; no rockets, no cash piles, no invented numeric results. Distinctly different from paper-cut, blueprints, 3D dashboards, gouache team scenes and split-screen comparisons. Important motifs inside central 80%, crop-resilient for blog cards. No extra words, no logos, no watermark, fully opaque light lilac background.

## Web Design That Converts: 7 Practical Rules

- Tool: built-in imagegen; opaque background.
- Final asset: `public/images/blogs/web-design-that-converts.webp`.
- Style: precision technical blueprint with orthographic wireframes.
- Subject: desktop and mobile layouts, seven numbered UX callouts, a primary
  CTA, page speed, testimonials, navigation and contact forms.
- Export: 1536 × 1024 WebP, quality 90.

### Generation prompt

Use case: ads-marketing. Asset type: editorial blog cover for Growthys article 'Web Design That Converts: 7 Practical Rules'. Create an elegant technical blueprint and precision drafting illustration, landscape 3:2. Dark navy blueprint canvas, fine white and cyan architectural drafting lines, warm orange selective highlights, precise orthographic front views, not 3D or isometric. A large centered desktop website wireframe pairs with a mobile website wireframe. The desktop has an obvious headline hierarchy, one strongly highlighted orange primary CTA, a simple navigation strip, three testimonial stars, and a short contact form; mobile shows generous touch targets and short form. Seven clean numbered circular drafting callouts 1 through 7 connect to meaningful details: headline clarity, single CTA, mobile layout, speed gauge, trust stars, navigation and contact button. Use only numerals 1 through 7 once each; the interface uses neat abstract lines rather than readable words. Thin dimension marks, alignment guides and a large outlined cursor arrow communicate thoughtful conversion-focused UX. Use a spacious horizontal composition, important devices stay within central 75% so square crops remain legible. Polished engineering drawing visual language, crisp technical strokes, subtle blueprint grain, no photos, no people, no paper-cut, no gouache, no glossy 3D, no split comparison panels, no logos, no watermark, no made-up statistics. Fully opaque background.

Use case: ads-marketing. Asset type: editorial cover for Growthys article 'HRMS Software: What It Is and Why Your Business Needs It'. ## Why Your Business Needs Custom CRM Software

- Tool: built-in imagegen; opaque background.
- Final asset: `public/images/blogs/custom-crm-software.webp`.
- Style: 3D isometric product diagram with frosted glass, ceramic and copper.
- Subject: custom lead pipeline, follow-up calendar, calls, reporting and
  connected email, chat, accounting and access-control modules.
- Export: 1536 × 1024 WebP, quality 90.

### Generation prompt

Use case: ads-marketing. Asset type: editorial cover for Growthys article 'Why Your Business Needs Custom CRM Software'. Create a premium 3D isometric product-diagram illustration, landscape 3:2. A modular custom CRM command center sits on a matte midnight-blue platform: a large central glass-and-ceramic dashboard with three clear sales pipeline columns containing individual lead profile cards, a calendar with a follow-up bell, a compact call log and a small manager analytics chart. Thin luminous connectors link the hub to separate email envelope, chat bubble, accounting ledger and role-access lock modules, making the idea of one connected customer management system immediately obvious. A few disorganized spreadsheet tiles on one side resolve into the organized pipeline; no arrows suggesting data leaves the system. Show bespoke modular software and automated follow-ups, not abstract futuristic technology. Sculptural dimensional objects, polished frosted glass, satin blue ceramic, warm copper accent details, tiny mint status accents, studio rim lighting and soft accurate shadows on a pale ice-blue background. This must be clearly different from paper-cut illustrations and flat split-screen comparison graphics. Refined architectural isometric composition, focus on the CRM modules and relationships. Central grouping stays legible in landscape and square crops. Only readable text is 'CRM' on the central platform; interface cards use abstract lines, no fabricated client names or numeric claims. No people, no robots, no storefronts, no brand logos, no watermark. Fully opaque background.

## Google Ads vs SEO: Which Should Your Business Choose?

- Tool: built-in imagegen; opaque background.
- Final asset: `public/images/blogs/google-ads-vs-seo.webp`.
- Style: flat 2D geometric screen-print comparison infographic, with halftone texture.
- Subject: paid search speed, clicks and budget versus organic search content,
  visibility and compounding growth; both support a shared business.
- Export: 1536 × 1024 WebP, quality 90.

### Generation prompt

Use case: ads-marketing. Asset type: editorial blog cover for 'Google Ads vs SEO: Which Should Your Business Choose?'. Make a premium flat 2D geometric screen-print infographic illustration, landscape 3:2, crisp vector-like shapes and restrained halftone texture, distinctly NOT paper-cut, NOT photoreal, NOT 3D. Balanced split composition: the left cobalt-blue panel depicts paid search as a bold orange lightning bolt, a cursor clicking a sponsored search card, and a small budget/coin symbol, conveying fast targeted leads requiring spend. The right warm ivory panel depicts organic search as a green staircase-shaped growing traffic line, a magnifying glass and well-organized content cards, conveying steady compounding growth. Both sides converge subtly toward the same small business target at the bottom center, showing complementary channels rather than a winner. Colors: cobalt, tangerine, deep forest green, ivory, charcoal. Smart editorial art direction, clean silhouette, strong readable composition with important motifs away from edges so it also crops well for square cards. The only text is the exact short labels 'GOOGLE ADS' on the left and 'SEO' on the right, bold clean sans serif. No numerical performance claims, no third party logos, no watermark, no other words. Fully opaque.

### Generation prompt

No people, no robots, no unrelated nature, no logos or watermarks, no readable text, no made-up numeric statistics. Fully opaque background.
